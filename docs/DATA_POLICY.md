# Data, privacy, and evidence scope

This is a curated, publishable subset of a much larger private research archive. It does not contain complete chat histories or all captured dumps. Reports, selected original project source, and compact primary output are included; coverage is listed in `research/INDEX.md`. Original files stay in place.

Copies remove local user paths, private network addresses, GPU UUIDs, boot UUIDs and known board serial forms. PCI BDFs are retained as local topology labels because the experiments depend on endpoint/root relationships; they are not universal addresses to copy into commands. Redaction prevents the public file from matching the original hash. `research/PROVENANCE.jsonl` gives both hashes and whether a transform occurred. Original hash lists from old experiments are not republished as checksums for changed copies.

Full firmware/SPI images and NVIDIA MODS/driver distributions are excluded. SPI pins and local-only patch builders are included instead. Missing original archive links become explicit textual references. Third-party firmware, tools, and linked source material remain subject to their respective terms. The small historical helper is a project-created executable, distributed unchanged with a pinned hash; exact source is unavailable. Do not infer open-source reproducibility from its inclusion.

Sanitization is pattern-based plus review of selected material, not a claim to prove that every possible private datum is recognizable. Machine-independent diagnostic logs generated by future runs should be reviewed before posting. Never upload keys, credentials, entire home directories, private conversations or full serial-bearing dumps.

The secondary-host archive directory is a public alias, not the original hostname. Provenance source paths use the same alias; original-byte SHA values remain unchanged. Board serial labels, chip IDs, ROM identity fields and known truncated identifiers are also redacted. A dedicated pattern scanner checks supported categories; its PASS is bounded to those patterns and does not prove that arbitrary data contains no identifying information.
